Ethnic Community Media and Audience Trust in America

How do communities find information they trust—and see their lives reflected in it? How do ethnic journalists work to build trust with their audiences? Our research examines the media serving ethnic and Indigenous communities, the relationships that sustain audience trust, and whose experiences remain visible in a changing information environment.

02

Relationships & belonging

Ethnic Media and Trust

These studies examine trust from both sides of the newsroom–audience relationship: how journalists make their reporting relevant to community life, and how immigrant audiences decide whether news speaks to them.

Journal article · 2026 · Journalism Practice

Credible, but Not for Me

Interviews with 22 Chinese, Korean, and Filipino immigrants reveal an ambivalent relationship with U.S. news: participants can see it as credible while also finding it alienating or propagandistic. The study examines how belonging and personal relevance shape trust and the turn toward alternative news sources.

Journal article · 2026 · Journalism & Mass Communication Quarterly

Contextualizing Trust: Geo-Ethnic Media, Relatable Storytelling, and Audience Trust

Drawing on interviews with 38 journalists, this study identifies three principles of trust-building: reporting from within, narrative stewardship, and reporting with cultural consciousness. It proposes “relatable journalism,” in which journalists and audiences recognize a shared sense of belonging.

MIDAS Lab report · 2024

Blurred Lines: When Immigrants Conflate News Stories and Propaganda

This public-facing report translates the immigrant interview research into recommendations for newsrooms. It explains why some Asian immigrant participants interpret U.S. coverage as propaganda and calls for reporting that better reflects their experiences, information needs, and perspectives on international affairs.

03

Community-rooted journalism

Ethnic Newsrooms’ Counter-Misinformation Work

How do newsrooms respond when false claims target the very communities they serve? This collaboration with the International Center for Journalists (ICFJ) examines the work of ethnic and Indigenous media around the 2024 U.S. presidential election.

Research report · October 2025 · In collaboration with ICFJ

Disarming Disinformation: United States

The report combines five outlet case studies, 45 journalist interviews, analysis of news and social media content, and a survey of 1,020 U.S. adults. It examines community-based responses to disinformation, including culturally informed reporting and efforts to address false narratives across languages and platforms. Its 22 recommendations identify actions for newsrooms and organizations supporting journalism.

The case studies feature ICT, New York Amsterdam News, The Haitian Times, Telemundo Yuma, and Phoenix North America Chinese Channel.

04

Representation in emerging information systems

Health Data Equity in AI for Racial Minority Communities

Broad racial categories can obscure differences in health needs. This study asks whether AI-generated health information recognizes those differences, focusing on the diversity within Asian American populations.

Conference article · 2025 · AIES

Disaggregated Health Data in LLMs: Evaluating Data Equity in the Context of Asian American Representation

The researchers evaluated GPT-3.5 responses for 21 Asian American subgroups, comparing the diversity and accuracy of the health information it produced. The study finds uneven representation and gaps in subgroup-specific health concerns, pointing to the need for more precise prompts and better integration of authoritative health data. The findings concern the model and data collection period studied, rather than all AI systems.
